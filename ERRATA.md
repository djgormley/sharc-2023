# Author-prepared errata

Paper: D. J. Gormley, “A Hybrid Technique to Increase Throughput of the Streaming Spectrum Sensor,” *2023 IEEE Space Hardware and Radio Conference (SHaRC)*, pp. 34–36, 2023. DOI: [10.1109/SHaRC56958.2023.10046267](https://doi.org/10.1109/SHaRC56958.2023.10046267).

Prepared: 2026-09-03

This document is an author-prepared errata, not an IEEE-issued correction. Page and equation references identify the three-page paper of record. The corrected manuscript is `output/pdf/sharc2023-fact-checked.pdf`; the detailed evidence and compliance audit is in `FACT_CHECK.md`.

## Corrections

1. **Page 1, (1): define the frequency normalization.** The factors printed as `exp(-j2πkn)` and `exp(-j2πvn)` are ambiguous because `k` and `v` are not defined as normalized frequencies. For physical trial frequencies `f_k` and `α_v` sampled at `f_s`, read these factors as `exp[-j2πf_k n/f_s]` and `exp[-j2πα_v n/f_s]`, respectively. The corrected manuscript also writes the filtering operation explicitly and denotes the finite-record estimate with a hat.

2. **Page 2, (3): correct and bound the DFT notation.** For zero-based DFT bin `m`, read the kernel as `exp(-j2πmn/N)` and the physical cycle frequency as `α_m = mf_s/N`. For the reported even transform length, the unique strictly positive bins are `m = 1, ..., N/2 - 1`; DC and the Nyquist bin are excluded. The supplemental RTL also suppresses bin 1 as CAF bleedover, so the reported `N = 2^16` implementation emits only bins 2 through 32,767. It emits each eligible above-threshold bin independently rather than grouping adjacent peaks. Off-grid leakage, multiple-bin testing, and the resulting family-wise false-alarm rate therefore require calibration.

3. **Page 1, Section III-A: correct the throughput arithmetic.** Reducing 11 downstream evaluations to two eliminates nine evaluations, an 81.8% reduction. It does not produce a ninefold speedup. Ignoring preprocessor cost, the upper bound for the illustrated serial-work ratio is `11/2 = 5.5`. Including the SRP cost makes the ratio smaller. The paper reports no serial-baseline measurement, so “increases throughput by a factor of nine” should be read as an unverified design motivation, not a result.

4. **Pages 1–2, Section III-A: correct attribution.** The instantaneous-power FFT/CAF preprocessor concept predates this paper. It is described by M. V. Koch and J. A. Downey, “Interference Mitigation Using Cyclic Autocorrelation and Multi-Objective Optimization,” NASA/TM–2019-220226, 2019, and is recommended in the author's 2021 thesis. The contribution claimed here is integration of that concept into the StSS architecture.

5. **Page 2, Section III-A: qualify the FFT-memory statement and record the saved configuration.** An `N`-point FFT requires order-`N` frame state, but it does not universally store all state in BRAM or wait for an entire frame before any processing. The supplemental snapshot contains a 65,536-point, 16-bit fixed-point FFT configured for pipelined-streaming I/O, unscaled arithmetic, convergent rounding, natural-order output, and block-RAM data and phase-factor storage. Its customization names a 125-MHz target, while its interface metadata and out-of-context constraint use 100 MHz. Its XCKU5P checkpoint is not the synthesis artifact behind Table I.

6. **Page 2, Sections III-B through III-D: qualify performance, endpoint, and reliability wording.** The two-tap result was an observation for earlier simulation cases; it does not “force periodogram convergence” generally. The supplemental snapshot retains 128 banks of 41 signed 16-bit FIR coefficients, generated at 488.28125-kHz bank spacing for a 125-MHz sample rate. It retains no before/after detection measurement supporting “dramatically improved” center-frequency accuracy. In the saved CFD, the sweep-wrap branch reports and resets the stored peak without comparing the final center-frequency result; the endpoint is omitted and the registered output alignment requires re-verification. The updated distributor is structurally simpler, but reliability was not measured. It has no active candidate backpressure, discards candidates when all CFDs are busy, and rejects new result writes when the result FIFOs are full. Its aggregate ready signal has inverted polarity, although the associated top-level feedback path is disabled.

7. **Page 3, Fig. 6 caption and following paragraph: correct the meaning of `β`.** `β` is a dimensionless threshold multiplier, not the probability of false alarm. `P_fa` is the resulting operating characteristic under a specified waveform, channel, search grid, filter, fixed-point, and decision configuration. The saved testbench quantizes `β = 1.23×10^-4` to the 16-bit value `8`, or `8/2^16`; its retained ROC script terminates without producing an ROC result. Additional CFD instances increase service capacity; they do not reduce the statistical precision needed to calibrate `β`.

8. **Page 3, Section IV and Table I: correct the FPGA part.** “Xilinx xc7k7-t” is not an identifiable device. The listed capacities—303,600 LUTs, 607,200 flip-flops, 1,030 36-Kb BRAMs, and 2,800 DSP slices—match the Virtex-7 **XC7VX485T**. The package and speed grade cannot be recovered and should not be inferred. The supplemental snapshot names four incompatible targets, and its only checkpoint is an FFT-only XCKU5P build whose 194.5 BRAM equivalents exceed the 186.5 reported for the complete design. It therefore cannot identify or validate the Table I build. The four printed utilization percentages are arithmetically correct.

9. **Page 3, Section IV: reconstruct and qualify the latency claim.** The supplemental RTL and testbench strongly explain 34.36 s as a calculated finest-grid latency. With `N = 2^16`, a 16-bit center-frequency word, minimum `FcStep = 1`, `f_s = 125 MHz`, one accepted sample per clock, and no stalls, a CFD evaluates `2^16` hypotheses of `2^16` samples each: `2^32/(125×10^6) = 34.359738368 s`. Two CFDs process separate candidates concurrently and do not divide this per-candidate sweep. The retained testbench instead uses `FcStep = 1638`; a separate four-CFD waveform shows a 22.544868-ms first result consistent with the same frame-count model and about two frames of startup. Thus 34.36 s is a well-supported conditional calculation, not a retained end-to-end measurement or demonstrated throughput gain. One two-CFD utilization point also cannot establish how BRAM scales with CFD count.

10. **Page 3, Section V: replace the conclusion.** The reported two-CFD counts are consistent with fitting an XC7VX485T, but the supplemental artifacts do not identify the build that produced them. The record does not demonstrate improved throughput, timing closure, power, reliability, maintained `P_d/P_fa`, or suitability for smaller edge devices. Those claims require new baseline-versus-hybrid measurements, an identified source revision, a portable self-checking testbench, and complete configuration and synthesis artifacts.

11. **Reference [1]: correct the thesis title.** The official title is “A Low-Memory Spectral-Correlation Analyzer for Digital QAM-SRRC Waveforms.” References should use complete venue data, DOI fields, and persistent online locations as shown in the corrected bibliography.
